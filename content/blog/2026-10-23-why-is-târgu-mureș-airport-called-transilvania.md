If you fly into central Romania, you may notice something slightly curious on your ticket: **Târgu Mureș Transilvania Airport**.

Târgu Mureș makes perfect sense — that is the city the airport serves. But *Transilvania*? Why give an airport the name of an entire region?

The answer is actually quite simple. **The airport is deliberately using the name of the region it serves.**

In May 2006, the Mureș County Council approved the name **“Transilvania Airport Târgu Mureș”**. The idea was to associate the airport with Transylvania, a name that is far better known internationally than Târgu Mureș itself.

And if you are arriving from abroad, that makes a certain amount of sense. Say “Târgu Mureș” to someone who has never been to Romania and they may struggle to place it on a map. Say “Transylvania”, and most people immediately know roughly where you mean.

The interesting part is that the airport itself is much older than the *Transilvania* name — and its aviation story goes back well before the modern terminal you see today.

## Almost 90 years of aviation history

The official history of Târgu Mureș Airport begins in **1936**, when an airport was established much closer to the city centre than today's facility.

The original site was only around 2.5 kilometres from central Târgu Mureș, on the western bank of the Mureș River.

But aviation had already arrived here more than two decades earlier.

On **4 September 1912**, Romanian aviation pioneer **Aurel Vlaicu** landed at the site in an aircraft he had designed and built himself. It is a remarkable detail to remember when you are travelling around modern Târgu Mureș: the city already had an aviation connection long before the airport itself was formally established.

Then came the Second World War.

In September 1944, retreating German forces destroyed the airport's terminal and hangar. The interruption, however, did not last forever. Flying resumed in **July 1945**, less than a year later.

That pattern — destruction, rebuilding and adaptation — would become part of the airport's story.

## The airport moved

If you are looking at the airport on a modern map, you are not looking at the place where that early aviation history happened.

Construction of a completely new airport began in **1961 near Vidrasău**, around 14 kilometres from Târgu Mureș. The new airport was formally inaugurated in **1969**, which is why that year is an important date in the history of the present-day facility.

Today, the airport sits at roughly **294 metres above sea level**, around 14.5 kilometres from Târgu Mureș.

Its runway is **2,000 metres long and 45 metres wide**, giving it the capacity to handle medium-sized passenger aircraft.

So when you land here today, you are arriving at a relatively modern airport — but one whose story stretches back through several generations of Romanian aviation.

## From regional airport to international gateway

For much of its existence, Târgu Mureș was principally a domestic and regional airport.

That began to change in the 2000s, when international airlines started appearing in its timetable.

**Wizz Air** became particularly important to the airport's development, while other international operators also joined the network. In **May 2012**, the airport received official certification as an international airport.

The following year, **Ryanair** began operating services to Brussels and Pisa.

Passenger numbers rose dramatically. Between 2010 and 2013, they increased by more than **380 per cent**. According to the airport's own historical account, this made Târgu Mureș the fastest-growing Romanian airport in terms of passenger numbers during that period.

There was another important chapter in **2018**, when the airport reopened to international traffic after major improvements to its runway.

For travellers, these developments may sound like dry airport history. In practical terms, though, they changed the role of Târgu Mureș completely.

It was no longer simply an airport for the people of Mureș County. It could increasingly function as an **international gateway into central Transylvania**.

## So why “Transilvania”?

This is the part that often prompts the question.

There is no mysterious historical figure called Transilvania associated with the airport. The airport was not named after a person, and *Transilvania* is not the name of the town or municipality where the terminal stands.

![](/uploads/imprejurimi-targu-mures.jpg)

It is a **regional identity**.

Târgu Mureș is in the historical region of Transylvania, and the airport's operators recognised that *Transylvania* has a much stronger international profile than the city's name alone.

For an overseas visitor, the name immediately provides geographical context:

**Târgu Mureș Transilvania Airport = you are arriving in Transylvania.**

There is also a practical reason for using the regional name. The airport is not intended to serve Târgu Mureș alone. Its position makes it useful for travellers heading towards other parts of central Romania, including the wider Transylvanian region.

And there is a small linguistic detail worth knowing.

**Transilvania** is the Romanian form of the name. In English, the region is normally called **Transylvania**.

So if you see *Aeroportul Transilvania Târgu Mureș* on a Romanian website or road sign, don't worry — you are looking at the same Transylvania you know in English.

## A few interesting facts about Târgu Mureș Transilvania Airport

* **IATA code:** TGM — taken from Târgu Mureș.
* **ICAO code:** LRTM.
* The present airport is in the **Vidrasău/Recea area**, administratively within Ungheni, rather than inside Târgu Mureș itself.
* The airport's aviation history reaches back to the **1930s**, while the present-day site dates from the 1960s and was inaugurated in 1969.
* The original airport site had a direct connection with **Aurel Vlaicu**, one of Romania's most important early aviation pioneers.
* In **1950**, the airport carried 4,350 passengers on the Târgu Mureș–Bucharest route during its first year of TARS operations.
* By **1960**, aircraft including the Li-2, Po-2, IAR 813, IAR 817 and Antonov An-2 were operating there, with 19,600 passengers handled that year.
* According to the airport's own historical record, it became the **first Romanian member of Airports Council International (ACI)**.

## A name that tells you where you are

So, why is Târgu Mureș Airport called **Transilvania**?

Because the name is doing more than identifying an airport. It is telling you something about the place you have just arrived in.

The airport belongs to Târgu Mureș, but the landscape, history and cultural world around it belong to a much larger region.

For an international traveller, *Transilvania* also does something very useful: it puts Târgu Mureș on the mental map.

You may arrive thinking you are simply flying to a Romanian city. Walk out of the terminal, and you are already in **Transylvania**.

And that, really, is the point of the name.

**It is a signpost as much as an airport name: you have arrived in Transylvania.**
